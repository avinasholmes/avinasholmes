Buddha from Lucknow

Recovered from the original Avinash Holmes website archive.


It was the month of June. After paddling the bicycle for 8 to 9 kilometers, Buddha was utterly tired and wanted to take a break under a tree. 

Drenched in sweat, he left the bicycle under the shade of a mango tree. He pulled out a piece of cloth and spread it on the ground. While doing so, he saw some mangoes on the ground and picked them up for eating while relaxing before the next part of his journey. 


Buddha from Lucknow was well known in his surrounding villages. As he was born on the day of Buddha Purnima (the day when actual Siddhartha Gautama- the Buddha, was born), his parents named him after the lord Buddha. Just like the Gautama Buddha, Buddha from Lucknow, too, found a way to live a happy life. 

Unlike Gautama Buddha, Buddha from Lucknow was born in poverty yet did not choose to run away from his responsibilities. So, he cared for his old parents, wife, two kids, and a herd of cows. 


After eating a few mangoes, Buddha thought of taking a nap before continuing his journey back to his home.

In an hour or so when Buddha woke up and saw a young man at some distance who was sitting in a squatting position with his hands folded and eyes gleaming in hope.

Who are you, and what do you seek? Buddha asked.

"Peace and happiness,” The man, who was some 30 years old, muttered.

Buddha, who gazed at him for a minute or two, spoke to him in a mysterious way- Unfortunately, your time for that is over, young man; you may go home now.

The man did not expect this answer; completely confused by what Buddha said, he stood up and asked- What…What do you mean?

Buddha, while peeling another mango, replied- I mean that you do not have many days left to live. 

The man came running to Buddha and held his feet.

"How can that be happening? I have not even started living my life; how could it be over so soon? Please do something. I want to live, please Buddha do something," he begged and cried.

Buddha looked at him again; he held his hand and, while looking at his palm, spoke calmly-

Well, you are not going to die tomorrow. As I can see, you still have one whole week to live, so it is up to you how you want to spend the last week of your life.

The man who was crying a moment before stood up, wiped his tears, looked at Buddha without uttering a word, took his bicycle, and ran back home.

Buddha could not hold his laughter; he knew what would happen next.

After a week, when Buddha was feeding his cows, he heard the sound of a bicycle that sounded familiar.

Soon came the same young man rushing to him, who now had just one more day to live. 

Strangely, now he appeared happy and with a smile on his face as if he had found a way to live forever.

He looked at the Buddha and started narrating his week.

He said he did not waste his time arguing with his wife, but he loved her more. He spent his time with his kids and money on things he and his family wanted without worrying about the future. 

During those six days, he did not worry about the future and chose to enjoy the present. He forgave everyone and asked for forgiveness from the people he had earlier issues with. Knowing that he only had a week to live, he gave up on jealousy, and he greeted even his enemies with a smile and showed affection to everyone he met.

After narrating everything, he took a deep breath and became quiet. 

It appears that you have finally learned how to live happily and peacefully. Buddha said.

The man nodded his head- yes.

Hmm... Bring me your palm, young man, Buddha asked.

The man stretched his hand towards the Buddha with curiosity.

Buddha pretended to look at the lines on his palm, and in a very somber tone, he said-

Something does not add up here!

What is that? The man asked curiously.

Buddha smiled and said- I could see that you have one more week to live.

The man looked at him confused; he was greatly dumbfounded and wondered if this was some mistake.

But I came to you a day before the week ended, as I wanted to die in your wise company, he muttered while looking at his palm.

Buddha, who was holding his smile, patted that young man's shoulder and asked him to go and live his life as he did in the past six days.

The man kept looking at him in bewilderment. 

As you know how to live happily now, you do not have to come back to me again once this week ends, said Buddha.

He did not understand what he meant, but he certainly knew how to spend his next week, so he left quietly. 

And Buddha went back to feeding his cows.
