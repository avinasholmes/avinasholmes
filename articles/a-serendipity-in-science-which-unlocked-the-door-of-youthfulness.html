A serendipity in science which unlocked the door of youthfulness

Recovered from the original Avinash Holmes website archive.

Serendipity in Science is like hitting a jackpot. Not everyone finds a jackpot in Biology probably because not everyone knows how to identify one. 

In general, four to five years are required for getting the highest degree- PhD, but in Life Sciences this is sometimes not enough. In the arena of research there is a constant battle between what you want and what life delivers to you. By life I mean the living organisms I am working with- Caenorhabditis elegans. No, it is not a typical scientific term or phenomenon but it is the name of the model organism, a tiny microscopic millimeter size transparent worm which I simply like to call noodle because of their noodle like appearance.

(This is how my model organism C. elegans looks under the microscope)

To put it in simple words- I am working on understanding why the muscle cells degenerate with age. Like every PhD project which begins with excitement and ends in frustration mine was no different. It was my dream project for sure and I believed that one day I will find the secret why muscle cells degenerate with time and design a formula to stop this degeneration and then we all can stay fit and fine-forever. Yes! Just like scientists working in a sci-fi movie, that's how excited and motivated I was in the first year of my PhD. Well, research life pushed a fast forward button and boom, three and a half years down the line all I know is that the mutant worm I made shows accelerated muscle degeneration. Sigh! I told you right in Biology chances are high that you more often end up getting the opposite of what you expect.

Exactly when everything was going down the hill, four months ago a jackpot hit me right in my eyes, like I said serendipity in science is like hitting a jackpot. 

It was a regular day in lab, I was maintaining my worm stocks which is basically picking worms from old plates and transferring them to new plate rich in food and nutrients necessary for their survival and reproduction.


Generally, this transfer is done once a week to replenish the food and to keep them alive and reproductively active to be able to use them for my future experiments.

There was a plate in my box with a date written on it. It was more than 40 days old plate. Out of curiosity to look at the fungus and other contaminations before I discard it into the biohazard bag, I looked at the plate through the microscope. 

Wait! What! Eureka?? No.. No… YES!!

I could not believe my eyes and the sight under the microscope. How? How is this possible? There were a bunch of worms expressing green fluorescent protein roaming around healthy as if they were a week old. 

I rushed to check my lab notebook and searched for the page with same date written on the plate. The date on plate was correct as there was an entry in my notebook. I was freaking out. Let me tell you why I was thrilled. If I compare, a 15-day old worm is equal to a 70 years old human being. By this, I mean by two weeks worms lose 80% of their muscle mass, less motile and reproductively inactive. The worms I saw under the microscope were like two day old worms, very much motile and were laying 100 of the eggs even they were 40 day old worms. 

I was checking this plate again and again. Flabbergasted! Yes, I was.

I called my PhD supervisor at 9PM and told her what I saw. I knew she was not going to believe it so, I clicked few images and sent to her on WhatsApp. Next day was Friday and its weekendhere. Upon having a look at the pictures, I sent her, she asked me to meet her in lab next day. 

She had a look at the worms and the entry in my lab notebook and she screamed in joy.

Go and get the wine from my office, she said.

Hell yeah! I said that in my mind.

She asked me to isolate the genomic DNA and send it for sequencing with primers against the fragment of DNA I inserted to suppress a repressor earlier. 

For people outside of Science, my supervisor asked me to find out what went wrong with this mutant by checking the DNA sequence.

My supervisor and I were waiting for the sequencing results. We got the results online in three days after the weekend and it was not the expected repressor gene that was hit by CRISPR but a part of DNA that encodes a very small protein called- Fudgy.

There is a human counterpart of this protein which shares 85% of sequence similarly with worm Fudgy protein and the function is unknown.

Coming back to our present time, two months after finding out reason why 40-day old worms were behaving like a 2-day old young worm, I flew to work in the primate lab of the National Institute of Health- Bethesda, United States. I discovered that at young age Fudgy protein is abundantly localized in human hair follicles however once you cross the age of 50 this protein starts to express in muscle cells and with time its expression increases.

I understood that this protein was causing muscle degeneration as the worms which were young and healthy had a non-functional Fudgy protein. 

Last week we filed a patent and signed a 100 million dollar deal with Lifetech company. 

The drug we made inhibits Fudgy protein in primates and humans as well. The primates with accelerated muscle degeneration were completely cured after they were administered with Fudgy inhibitor. Also, worms administered with Fudgy inhibitor in their food survived for 90 days which is equivalent to 420 years of human age.

Well the serendipity has not only changed my life forever but it is going to change the life of human race in future as Lifetech company is going to begin its trial on humans next month. 

By the way I have been invited as a guest speaker, this time at the MIT.
