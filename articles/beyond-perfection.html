Beyond Perfection

Recovered from the original Avinash Holmes website archive.

In pursuit of perfection, I lost touch with the raw essence of my art—the visions only I could see and create. Now, I return to drawing as I once did, letting my imagination flow freely, uninfluenced, imperfect, and truly mine.

This piece reflects a journey back to creativity’s roots, where art transcends replication and finds life in originality.


As a kid, I drew what I saw,
Sketches born from my raw imagination.
My drawings were visions only I could think,
Only I could see; no one else glimpsed these things
Until my pencil met the page.
They were my ideas, my thoughts—
Novel and new to the world.

Then I grew up, went to school,
And I began to copy art.
I sketched familiar faces,
Painted scenes everyone knew at a glance.
I chased perfection in every line,
Erasing and erasing, until each shape was flawless.
People were awed by the resemblance,
By the perfection—and I liked that.

But now, at thirty-five, I look back at those perfect drawings,
And I feel something I missed
While I ran after perfection.
I don’t see imagination there;
All I see is replication,
Reflections of what already exists.
I see effort and hard work,
But not something original,
Not something nascent,
Something the world hadn’t seen
Before my pencil touched the blank canvas.

So now, I’ve started drawing again—
Things I see, things I imagine.
Imperfect, vulnerable, raw on the first attempt,
Thought-provoking, unique,
Art that only my mind can see,
Uninfluenced by anyone else.
