Molecules and Life

Recovered from the original Avinash Holmes website archive.

What is life? A fleeting spark or the sum of the lifeless? Perhaps we are the universe looking back at itself in awe—wondering, questioning—before dissolving into the cosmic dust once more. 



They say the coal is dead and cold,
The air is lifeless, still and old.
The water flows but holds no soul,
No beating heart, no living role.

I stand within my laboratory space,

Where amino acids and nucleotides rest in vials,

Phospholipids and glucose lie still—

Silent, motionless—surely unalive.

Dead, they say—no breath, no sound,
Yet in the cell, the same are found.
The same raw threads of life's design,
Yet now they pulse, they twist, they bind.

Nucleotides in tangled strands,
Woven tight by unseen hands.
Within the cell, the code of life,

Yet in the vials, inert and cold.

Nucleotides weave the strands of DNA,

The essence of life, or so they say.

You can hold it in your hands,

Show it to the world,

Yet inside a tube, lifeless, inert. 

So, what is life? The dust that breathes?
A fleeting glow before it leaves?
If all that's living is born from the dead,
Then does the dead hold life instead?

Perhaps we are but fleeting sparks of matter,
The dust that breathes in cosmic dark.

Gaining consciousness,

Asking what it means to be—

Before returning to the great cosmic jar.

Or we break down—amino acids, nucleotides—

Resting in vials, silent and stark,

Waiting to rise again one day,

To watch the universe through borrowed eyes.
