Epigenetics: Shaping Your Genes for a Better Tomorrow

Recovered from the original Avinash Holmes website archive.

I’ve always believed that the stories we tell ourselves are more powerful than the facts we know. As a psychotherapist, I’ve spent years helping people reshape their narratives, helping them see that they are more than the sum of their experiences and their genes. But the story I’m about to share is not just a case study or a clinical example. It’s my story, my family’s story—a story of how one man’s determination and the power of epigenetics changed the course of our lives.

My father, Dr. Rajan Kumar, was not just a brilliant molecular biologist; he was a man who understood the profound impact of the unseen forces that shape us. He knew that our genes—those tiny sequences of DNA that we inherit from our parents—were not our destiny. And this understanding would become the cornerstone of his life’s work and the legacy he passed on to me.

My father was born into a world of struggle. His father, my grandfather, had grown up in extreme poverty, facing hardships that left deep scars on his psyche. The stress of daily survival, coupled with the trauma of a difficult childhood, manifested as anxiety and depression—an inheritance that was passed down to my father. As a child, I often saw my father battle with these demons. He was a man of intellect, but his mind was a battlefield.

It wasn’t until my father began his doctoral studies in molecular biology that he started to see a way out of this cycle. He was already deeply interested in genetics, the study of how traits are passed down from one generation to the next. But as he delved deeper into his research, he became aware of a new and exciting field—epigenetics.

Epigenetics, as my father would later explain to me, is like a set of switches that can turn genes on or off. Imagine you’ve inherited a blueprint for building a house, but depending on how you live your life—your diet, your environment, your thoughts and emotions—different parts of that blueprint get highlighted, modified, or even ignored. My father realized that while he had inherited the genes that predisposed him to anxiety and depression, he could change how those genes were expressed.

This revelation was life-changing for my father. He saw a way to break the cycle that had plagued our family for generations. But he also knew that it would require a complete transformation of his lifestyle—a journey that he would embark on with the same determination that had driven his scientific career.

He began with meditation. Every morning, before the sun had even risen, he would sit in stillness, focusing on his breath. It wasn’t easy at first; his mind was a whirlwind of thoughts, worries, and fears. But over time, the practice began to have a calming effect. The anxiety that had once gripped him so tightly began to loosen its hold.


Next, my father turned to yoga. He found a small studio near our home and began attending classes regularly. The physical practice was demanding, but it also taught him the importance of connecting his mind and body. Through yoga, he learned to release the tension that had built up over years of stress. The combination of meditation and yoga started to reshape not just his body, but his mind as well.

But my father knew that true change required more than just physical and mental practices. He needed to address every aspect of his life. He began prioritizing sleep, ensuring that he got a full night’s rest each night. He changed his diet, cutting out processed foods and incorporating more whole foods, fresh fruits, and vegetables. He also made regular exercise a priority, finding joy in running, weightlifting, and hiking.

Perhaps the most significant change my father made was in his approach to work. As a scientist, he had always been driven by his research, often working late into the night and sacrificing his personal life for the sake of his career. But he realized that this approach was unsustainable. He began to set boundaries, leaving the lab at a reasonable hour, and making time for hobbies and socializing. He reconnected with old friends, spent more time with our family, and even started pursuing creative interests.

Years passed, and my father’s transformation was nothing short of remarkable. The man who had once been consumed by anxiety and depression was now calm, confident, and happy. He had broken free from the genetic chains that had bound him for so long, and he felt ready to start a family of his own.

When my father and my mother, Meera, decided to have children, he did so with a sense of peace. He knew that he had done everything in his power to ensure that his children would not inherit the same struggles he had. He had rewritten his genetic legacy through the power of epigenetics, and with it, the future of our family.

Growing up, I always knew that my father was different from other parents. He was deeply involved in our lives, always there to support us, to listen, and to guide us. But it wasn’t until I became a psychotherapist that I truly understood the depth of the work he had done—not just for himself, but for us, his children.

As I sit here, writing the final chapter of this book, I can’t help but feel a deep sense of gratitude for the man who gave me so much more than just life. He gave me the tools to live that life to its fullest, free from the shadows that had haunted our family for generations.

This book is not just a guide for overcoming trauma, anxiety, and depression through epigenetics—it’s a tribute to my father, Dr. Rajan Kumar, and the legacy he has left behind. His story is a testament to the idea that we are not prisoners of our genetics. We have the power to change, to heal, and to pass down a legacy of mental wellness to those who come after us.

And so, I leave you with this thought: The story you tell yourself is the most important story of all. Choose to write a story of hope, of resilience, and of transformation. Just as my father did, you too can break the cycle and create a new legacy for yourself and your children.
